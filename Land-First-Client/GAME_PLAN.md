# Game Plan — Landing Client #1

## The core problem to solve

Not "not enough skills" — you have real, shipped proof across a wide range (e-commerce, admin systems, CRM, CMS, security, SEO). The actual problems are:

1. **Positioning is too broad.** "Web dev, redesigns, mobile, CRMs, HRIS, inventory, school systems, SEO/GEO, Kiosk POS, and more" is a capability list, not a pitch. Nobody hires "does everything" — they hire "does the thing I need, and has proof of it."
2. **Your best proof isn't visible.** Acme Vintage Supply — the most recent, most complete, real-client build — isn't on the portfolio at all yet.
3. **No pricing shown.** Prospects can't self-qualify; every lead requires a full conversation just to find out if you're in their budget.
4. **Zero reviews/track record on freelance platforms** (Upwork specifically) — a new account with no history converts far worse than one with 2–3 reviews, regardless of skill.

None of these are slow fixes. All four are closeable in days, not months.

---

## Step 1 — Fix positioning (do this before sending anything)

Keep the full service list on the site as capability — that's fine, it shows range. But for any individual pitch (proposal, cold email, DM), lead with **one** service and **one** matching proof point:

| Their visible need | Lead service | Proof to show |
|---|---|---|
| Outdated/broken business site | Site Rescue / Redesign | Acme Sign / Frasco rebuilds |
| Needs an internal tool/dashboard | System Design (CRM, inventory, HRIS) | **Paul CRM** — live, real, built solo |
| Retail / restaurant / self-serve | Kiosk POS | kiosk-pos project |
| School / org needs a system | Management System | School Management System case study |
| Invisible on Google | SEO/GEO | Acme's SEO pass (JSON-LD, sitemap, structured data) |
| Wants "someone who can build the whole thing" | Full-stack build | **Acme Vintage Supply** — storefront + admin + CMS + security + marketing, solo, ~7 weeks |

Mobile (Expo) has no shipped proof yet — keep it listed as a capability, don't lead a pitch with it until there's a demo build to point to.

---

## Step 2 — Close the two visibility gaps

1. Write up **Acme Vintage Supply** as a real case study (see `CASE_STUDY_ACME_VINTAGE_SUPPLY.md`) and get it live on the portfolio. This is the single highest-leverage item in this whole plan — it's your strongest, most recent, most relatable proof, and right now nobody can see it.
2. Decide on pricing visibility (see `PRICING_PACKAGES.md`) — either show "starting at $X" to pre-qualify leads, or keep it hidden deliberately to force a discovery call. Pick one on purpose, don't leave it as an accidental gap.

## Step 3 — Channel sequencing (run in parallel, not sequentially)

### OnlineJobs.ph — start here first
Built specifically for PH freelancers targeting US/CA/AU employers — matches your exact situation. Employers browse and message first here more than they do on Upwork, so a strong, specific profile does passive work for you. Post as a specialist ("Next.js Developer — Business Sites, CRMs & Dashboards"), not a generalist.

### Upwork — apply, but bootstrap deliberately
New accounts convert badly on high-ticket work with zero reviews. Don't lead with System Design ($4,500+) here first.
- First 1–2 jobs: target small **Landing Page Sprint** or **Site Rescue** gigs, priced to win your first reviews, not to profit.
- Once you have 2–3 five-star reviews: pivot toward CRM/system-design proposals where Paul CRM becomes the differentiator.

### Indeed — bridge income, not a replacement
You need cash flow now; outbound and freelance platforms take weeks to convert. Full-time or contract roles here aren't a trap — they don't stop you from also freelancing on the side.

### Your own outreach — the real differentiator
20–30 real, hand-picked, individually-researched prospects (see the standing rule about the 51k list — not that list). This is the only channel where you're not competing against a wall of other bids. Personalize every message around something specific and true about their actual site/business.

## Step 4 — Weekly cadence

**This week:**
- Apply to matching Indeed listings (Next.js/SEO roles are the best fit for your stack)
- Finish OnlineJobs.ph profile
- Submit 3–5 Upwork proposals on small entry-tier jobs
- Ship the Acme case study page

**Ongoing, daily 30–60 min block:**
- Research and add 3–5 real prospects to Paul CRM's pipeline
- Send 3–5 personalized outreach messages

**Weekly review:**
- Look at what got replies vs. silence
- Adjust the *pitch angle* (which service/proof combo is landing), not just the volume
